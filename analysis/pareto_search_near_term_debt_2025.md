The seeded search scored 5,000 designs; 4,849 balanced within the 10%-60% headline-rate range, and 119 were nondominated.
The net-revenue target is $5,450.9B, including $492.2B (1.6% of GDP) above tax-replacement neutrality.
Headline/business/top rates are restricted to 0.5% steps and rounded up to the first target-meeting rate; statutory middle wage rates are rounded to the nearest step. The resulting candidate surpluses range from $0.0B to $111.3B.
The modeled current ESI exclusions are worth $410.0B in combined federal income and payroll taxes, or about $2,478 per ESI-covered person. The health amounts below are true refundable credits, not income exclusions, and are deliberately searched near that tax-value scale.

## Reading the balanced design

The equal-weight compromise is candidate 1570: a 35.0% headline/business rate, no statutory zero bracket, a 24.5% middle rate through $80,000 per schedule adult, and the headline rate above that threshold. It combines a $4,000 adult nonrefundable credit, no refundable adult wage credit, a $2,000 refundable child credit, and a $3,250 adult / $750 child refundable health credit.
The discrete rate raises $5.4B above the modeled target.

Its health-credit score is $474.0B: $442.2B for the ESI transition plus $13.0B for nongroup enrollees without APTC, $7.7B of APTC floor top-ups, and $11.1B for induced enrollment at 15% take-up.

Its adult-relief architecture is nonrefundable credit. The nonrefundable credit makes the effective zero-net-wage-tax threshold $16,327 per adult. The refundable wage credit has no earnings phase-in.

At the default assumptions, 78.1% of ESI-covered people are in non-losing tax units; the covered-person median annual resource change is $7,327, the 10th percentile is -$5,424, and the mean absolute MTR change is 3.6 percentage points (68.3% remain within two points).

The stress case uses 75% employer-health wage pass-through and raises the benchmark factor from 1.03 to 1.15. See the JSON for decile results, exact fiscal identities, scope limits, and the full frontier.

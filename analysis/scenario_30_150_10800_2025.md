# X-tax scenario: 25/30/35 wage rates, $10,800 child credit

**Benchmark:** provisional 2025 U.S. accounts and tax rules. Fiscal figures are annual billions of 2025 dollars, before behavioral responses. GDP and wages are long-run real percentage differences relative to that benchmark. This is an illustrative model score, not an official estimate.

## Policy scored

- Wage tax: 25% on taxable employer compensation up to $75,000 per filing adult, 30% from $75,000 to $150,000 per filing adult, and 35% above $150,000. The married thresholds are $150,000 and $300,000 per return. Business destination-based cash-flow tax: 35% with immediate expensing. No tax exclusion for employer-paid insurance, pensions, other benefits, or employer payroll contributions; model employer compensation as cash.
- Repeal federal individual income, payroll, corporate income, and customs taxes. Replace their existing refundable income-tax credit outlays with the credits below.
- Earned adult credit: $2,000 per eligible adult, phased in at 10% of compensation and never phased out. Fully refundable child credit: $10,800 per child, with no age supplement or earnings phase-in.
- Insurance purchase credit: $3,000 per eligible adult under 65 and $1,500 per child. Main case **retains existing ACA premium assistance** and only tops it up to the flat credit where required, as well as adding the credit to other eligible coverage. The alternative replaces the existing ACA premium tax credit, as in the current website default. The health model assumes 15% take-up among currently uninsured eligible people.
- No repeal of SNAP, Medicaid, housing assistance, or other separately modeled social programs; no fiscal savings claimed from them. The $10,800 child credit is an addition to those programs.

## Score

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Long-run real GDP | **+0.95%** | **+0.95%** * |
| Long-run real pretax wage per hour | **+0.84%** | **+0.84%** * |
| Estimated work hours | +0.11% | +0.11% * |
| Static new tax receipts, after new credits | **$5,126.8B** | $5,077.1B |
| Receipts from four replaced taxes | $5,051.3B | $5,051.3B |
| Existing refundable tax-credit outlays removed | $92.6B | $92.6B |
| Existing ACA assistance removed | $0 | $105.8B |
| **Static annual reduction in deficit** | **$168.1B** | **$224.2B** |

\* The labor simulator currently treats the ACA credit as a flat purchase credit in its average tax comparison, so it does not recompute the labor response for retaining versus replacing ACA premium subsidies. The real GDP and wage figures are shared illustrative estimates, not separately simulated results for the two ACA treatments.

The main fiscal calculation is **$6,740.4B gross tax − $366.0B adult credit − $777.8B child credit − $469.7B new health credits = $5,126.8B** in reform receipts. Subtract $5,051.3B in replaced tax receipts and add $92.6B in eliminated refundable tax-credit outlays to get **$168.1B** of annual deficit reduction. Existing ACA assistance remains in the current-law spending baseline; the $469.7B health-credit estimate includes only incremental top-ups for ACA recipients.

If ACA premium subsidies are replaced, health credits cost $519.5B, of which $105.8B in existing ACA assistance is retired; the fiscal advantage over retention is $56.1B. All other transfer spending remains intact. The comparison holds insurance enrollment assumptions fixed.

## Labor and output calculation

Using 76,652 public CPS tax units projected to 2025 cash wages, the earnings-weighted *federal tax* marginal wedge changes from **30.69% to 30.50%**. Approximately **46.3% of workers** in the sample have a lower estimated marginal tax rate and **53.7%** have a higher one. Current-law EITC phase-ins and phase-outs, the Social Security earnings cap, household filing status, and the replacement of payroll taxes account for the variation. The new statutory wage tax is 15% while the 10% adult credit is phasing in against the 25% bracket, on the model's eligible compensation definition.

The illustrative labor equation adds a **+0.415% substitution response** and a **−0.305% income response**, for **+0.110% hours**. Under the simple open-economy capital model, full expensing with the 35% business tax raises the capital-to-labor ratio enough for **+0.837% real pretax wage per hour**. Multiplying hours by output per hour gives **+0.948% real GDP**, or about $292B at the provisional 2025 GDP level. The model calibrates one capital response to [Tax Foundation's distinct 21% DBCFT Option 71](https://taxfoundation.org/tax-reform-guide/option/replace-the-corporate-income-tax-with-a-destination-based/); it does not reproduce the Tax Foundation's full output or wage estimate for this reform. [CBO's labor microsimulation description](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62267) motivates using household-level tax changes and separate behavioral elasticities.

These are **pretax** wage effects. A higher child credit increases disposable income but does not alter the statutory marginal wage-tax rate, while the credit's income effect modestly reduces estimated hours.

## Interpretation and limits

The $168B is a **static** annual fiscal score: it does not include the GDP feedback, transition rules, current-law tax receipts that would also change with GDP, government debt feedback, or revenue from future investments. Simply scaling the new gross tax base with the GDP estimate would add about $64B of *illustrative* reform receipts, but that shortcut is not a valid full dynamic deficit score.

The labor simulation preserves the modeled social programs in both cases but omits their benefit withdrawal rates, work-entry decisions by current nonworkers, and the income effect of ACA top-ups or ACA replacement. Its current-law tax calculator is an approximate 2025 wage-only calculation, not tax-return microsimulation. It assumes all sampled workers obtain the insurance purchase credit and full conversion of employer-paid benefits and employer payroll taxes into taxable compensation. The fiscal health model instead uses coverage and enrollment weights; that difference especially affects the labor-income effect. These omissions matter to the +0.95% GDP estimate, while the accounting behind the static fiscal estimate is directly reproducible.

Run the fiscal and capital checks with `npm test -- --reporter=verbose tests/scenario_30_150_10800.test.ts`.

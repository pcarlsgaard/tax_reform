# Expanded 2025 X-tax and health-credit Pareto search — Current-law static baseline

The seeded search scored 5,000 designs; 4,936 balanced within the 10%-60% headline-rate range, and 123 were nondominated.
The current-law static replacement baseline is $4,958.7B.
Headline/business/top rates are restricted to 0.5% steps and rounded up to the first fiscal-floor-meeting rate; statutory middle wage rates are rounded to the nearest step. The resulting static deficit reductions range from $0.0B to $111.5B.
The modeled current ESI exclusions are worth $410.0B in combined federal income and payroll taxes, or about $2,478 per ESI-covered person. The health amounts below are true refundable credits, not income exclusions, and are deliberately searched near that tax-value scale.

## Reading the balanced design

The equal-weight compromise is candidate 1156: a 35.0% headline/business rate, a $20,000 zero bracket per schedule adult, a 24.5% middle rate through $80,000 per schedule adult, and the headline rate above that threshold. It combines a $0 adult nonrefundable credit, no refundable adult wage credit, a $8,000 refundable child credit, and a $3,500 adult / $0 child refundable health credit.
At the discrete rate, the plan reduces the static deficit by $32.5B relative to current law.

Its health-credit score is $478.2B: $445.1B for the ESI transition plus $13.4B for nongroup enrollees without APTC, $8.2B of APTC floor top-ups, and $11.4B for induced enrollment at 15% take-up.

Its adult-relief architecture is zero bracket. The statutory zero bracket is $20,000 per schedule adult. The refundable wage credit has no earnings phase-in.

At the default assumptions, 89.8% of ESI-covered people are in non-losing tax units; the covered-person median annual resource change is $12,351, the 10th percentile is -$125, and the mean absolute MTR change is 3.6 percentage points (68.3% remain within two points).

The stress case uses 75% employer-health wage pass-through and raises the benchmark factor from 1.03 to 1.15. See the JSON for decile results, exact fiscal identities, scope limits, and the full frontier.
